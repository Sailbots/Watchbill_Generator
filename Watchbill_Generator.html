    Quick-start tutorial

    The short version: tell the app who you have and what watches exist, say which days belong to which duty section, then let it build the month. Work through the steps in order the first time. Everything on the app comes pre-loaded with made-up example data, so you can click around freely.

    1. Add sailors
    Management → Sailors
    • Type a name, pick a department, and press Add sailor. Names and departments can be edited right in the table.
    • Each column is a watchstation. Check the box for every watch that person is qualified to stand. The app will never assign someone to a watch they aren't checked for.
    • The ✕ at the end of a row removes that person. Use the filter box to find someone in a long roster.
    • Delete the example sailors when you're ready to enter your own.
    2. Set up your watches and hours
    Management → Watchstations
    • Duty day: set when a normal workday duty day starts and ends, and the hours used for weekends and holidays.
    • Departments: add or remove the departments your people belong to.
    • Watchstations: add the watches you stand and choose how long each one is (for example 4-hour rotating watches, or all-day watches that one person holds for the whole duty day). Choose which departments can stand each one.
    • Saved structures lets you save the current set of watches under a name so you can switch layouts later.
    3. Split people into duty sections
    Management → Duty Sections
    • Add as many sections as your command uses (1, 2, 3…).
    • Under Assign a sailor, start typing a name, pick the section, and press Assign. Assigning someone who is already in a section moves them.
    • The section rosters below show who is in each section, so you can check that every section has enough people.
    4. Standby qualifications (optional)
    Management → Qualified Standbys

    Some commands need certain qualified people on the watchbill each duty day even when those watches are not actively manned. Add each of those as a standby watch, then check which sailors hold it. The coverage panel at the top shows whether each section has someone for every standby. If your command doesn't use this, skip it.

    5. Build the month
    Duty Calendar → Calendar Editor
    • Pick the month, year and a duty rotation.
    • Add anything that takes people out of being eligible certian days. Under Off-Duty Entry, enter a name, dates, and a short reason, like "Leave" or "TAD". Under Holidays & Off Days, enter days for weekend hours.
    • Press Set duty rotation. This only decides which section has the duty each day
    • Press Generate month. The app fills every day's watchbill, following rules like no back-to-back watches, no back-to-back duty days, only qualified sailors, etc. and spreads hours as evenly as it can.
    • Any day it couldn't fill cleanly is flagged, with a plain explanation of why (for example, not enough qualified people that day).
    6. Review, adjust, and print
    Calendar → click a day → Open in editor
    • Click any day on the calendar to preview it, then Open in editor to change it.
    • In the Watchbill Editor, every cell is a drop-down of the people who can stand that watch. Change anyone you want. The readout below the table warns you if a change breaks a rule.
    • Fill from duty section rebuilds that single day; Clear assignments empties it; Reset lengths puts the watch times back to default.
    • The undo arrow (↶) in the top bar steps back one change at a time.
    • For paper copies, open Watchbill Printoff (one day) or Calendar Printoff (whole month) and press Print. The signature lines at the bottom can be changed in App Settings.
    7. Save your work
    Gear icon → App Settings
    • Turn on Autosave to keep your work in this browser. It stays on this computer only; it is never uploaded anywhere.
    • Clearing your browser's site data erases it. To be safe, press Export everything now and then and keep the file. Import from file restores it, including on a different computer.
    • Watchbills older than 30 days are cleared automatically, so export a month before it ages out if you need to keep it.

    About/Contact me!

    I use AI/ML to try and solve problems for everyday sailors. Nothing here represents opinions of the Navy, the DoW, or my command. This is not a program of record, nor endorsed by the US Navy. For issues, feedbag, questions/comments, feel free to reach out to me at james.m.coleman165.mil@us.navy.mil "Standby Watchstations" were made with Nuclear OP B.5 requirements in mind, but are likely relevant to other commands. Ensures people who have certain quals will be on a watchbill, even if those watches are not stood up. The program will also throw errors for back to back watches, back to back duty days, not enough qualified personnel, and is written to maximize an equitable distribution of watchstanding hours among sailors. Delete the "example" sailors, watchstations, etc. to implement your own.
